## 1. Governance

### 1.1 Board-approved fraud risk policy
NBFCs must have a board-approved policy on fraud risk management that defines the roles and responsibilities of the board, its committees and senior management, and lays down measures for prevention, early detection, investigation, staff accountability, monitoring, recovery and reporting of frauds.

### 1.2 Special committee for monitoring frauds
A board-level committee must oversee the effectiveness of the fraud risk management framework, review and monitor cases of fraud, and ensure that the root causes of frauds are identified and corrective action taken.

## 2. Early Warning Signals and Red Flagged Accounts

### 2.1 Early Warning Signal framework
NBFCs must put in place a framework of Early Warning Signals (EWS) for early detection and prevention of fraud, integrated with their core systems. Indicative EWS include early payment default soon after disbursement, frequent changes in the borrower's declared profile, inconsistency between declared income and observed account credits, and unusual concentration of stressed accounts sourced through a single intermediary.

### 2.2 Red Flagged Accounts
An account in which an EWS indicates possible fraudulent activity must be classified as a Red Flagged Account (RFA). The NBFC must complete an examination of an RFA, including a forensic audit where warranted, within a board-approved timeline and decide whether to classify the account as fraud.

## 3. Classification and reporting of fraud

### 3.1 Principles of natural justice
Before classifying an account as fraud, the NBFC must issue a show-cause notice to the persons concerned detailing the transactions and grounds, give a reasonable time of not less than 21 days to respond, and pass a reasoned order after considering the response.

### 3.2 Reporting to the Reserve Bank
Frauds must be reported to the Reserve Bank through the prescribed Fraud Monitoring Return within 14 days from the date of classification of an incident or account as fraud.

### 3.3 Reporting to law enforcement
Frauds must be reported to the appropriate law enforcement agency as specified in the directions, subject to applicable thresholds, without undue delay after classification.
