## 1. Applicability and layers

### 1.1 Layered structure
NBFCs are regulated in four layers according to size, activity and perceived riskiness: Base Layer (NBFC-BL), Middle Layer (NBFC-ML), Upper Layer (NBFC-UL) and a notionally empty Top Layer. Regulatory requirements are cumulative: an NBFC in a higher layer must comply with the requirements of the lower layers as well as those specific to its own layer.

### 1.2 Base Layer
The Base Layer comprises non-deposit taking NBFCs with an asset size below Rs 1,000 crore, and NBFCs undertaking specified low-risk activities such as peer-to-peer lending platforms, account aggregators and non-operative financial holding companies.

### 1.3 Middle Layer
The Middle Layer comprises all deposit-taking NBFCs irrespective of asset size, and non-deposit taking NBFCs with an asset size of Rs 1,000 crore and above. Standalone primary dealers, infrastructure debt funds, core investment companies, housing finance companies and infrastructure finance companies are also placed in the Middle Layer.

### 1.4 Upper Layer
The Upper Layer comprises NBFCs specifically identified by the Reserve Bank as warranting enhanced regulatory requirements based on a scoring methodology of size, interconnectedness, complexity and supervisory inputs. The top ten eligible NBFCs by asset size always reside in the Upper Layer.

## 2. Asset classification and provisioning

### 2.1 NPA recognition norm
An asset is classified as a non-performing asset when principal or interest remains overdue for more than 90 days. Base Layer NBFCs transitioned to the 90-day norm through a glide path: overdue for more than 150 days by March 2024, more than 120 days by March 2025 and more than 90 days by March 2026.

### 2.2 Special Mention Accounts
Lenders must identify incipient stress by classifying accounts as Special Mention Accounts: SMA-0 where principal or interest is overdue for 1 to 30 days, SMA-1 for 31 to 60 days and SMA-2 for 61 to 90 days. Classification is done as part of the day-end process and upgradation of an NPA to standard requires repayment of all arrears of interest and principal.

### 2.3 Standard asset provisioning
NBFCs must hold a provision on standard assets as prescribed for their layer and must not net such provisions from gross advances. Provisioning on NPAs follows the age of the NPA (sub-standard, doubtful, loss).

## 3. Governance requirements

### 3.1 Board-approved policies (all layers)
Every NBFC must have a board-approved policy on grant of loans to directors, senior officers and their relatives, a board-approved policy on internal capital and risk, and a Risk Management Committee of the board or at executive level that reports to the board.

### 3.2 Chief Compliance Officer (Middle Layer and above)
NBFC-ML and NBFC-UL must appoint a Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) of sufficient seniority, generally not below two levels below the CEO, with a minimum fixed tenure of three years. The CCO must not be given any business targets and removal before tenure requires board approval and reporting to the Reserve Bank.

### 3.3 Compliance function
NBFC-ML and above must put in place a board-approved compliance policy that sets out the compliance philosophy, the structure and role of the compliance function, and the processes for identifying, assessing, monitoring and reporting compliance risk. The compliance function must be independent and sufficiently resourced.

### 3.4 Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process
NBFC-ML and above must carry out an Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) on the same lines as banks, proportionate to scale and complexity, to assess capital needed for risks not captured in the regulatory minimum.

## 4. Disclosures

### 4.1 Disclosures in financial statements (Middle Layer and above)
NBFC-ML and above must disclose in their annual financial statements, among other items: exposures by sector including real estate and capital market; intra-group exposures; unhedged foreign currency exposure; related party disclosures; customer complaints received and disposed of, including top grounds of complaint; breach of covenants of loans availed or debt securities issued; and divergence in asset classification and provisioning where the divergence exceeds the specified threshold.

### 4.2 Corporate governance report
NBFCs with public shareholding or in the Middle Layer and above must publish a corporate governance report covering the composition of the board and its committees, the number of meetings held, details of directors' remuneration, and any penalties imposed by regulators.

## 5. Concentration and exposure

### 5.1 Credit concentration norms (Middle Layer)
An NBFC-ML must limit its exposure to a single counterparty and to a group of connected counterparties relative to its Tier 1 capital, within the ceilings specified by the Reserve Bank, and must have a board-approved policy on exposure to sensitive sectors. Exposures above the ceiling require specific board approval and reporting.

### 5.2 Sensitive sector exposures
NBFCs must have board-approved limits on exposure to capital markets and commercial real estate, and must report such exposures in the prescribed returns.

### 5.3 Loans to directors and senior officers
Loans to directors, their relatives, and entities in which directors are interested above specified thresholds require sanction by the board and must be disclosed in the annual financial statements.
