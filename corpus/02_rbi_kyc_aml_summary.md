## 1. Governance of the KYC/AML programme

### 1.1 Board-approved KYC policy
Regulated entities must have a board-approved KYC policy covering the customer acceptance policy, risk management, customer identification procedures and monitoring of transactions.

### 1.2 Designated Director and Principal Officer
A Designated Director must be nominated to ensure overall compliance with obligations under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act and Rules. A Principal Officer, at management level, is responsible for ensuring compliance, monitoring transactions, and sharing and reporting information to the Financial Intelligence Unit - India (FIU-IND).

## 2. Customer due diligence

### 2.1 Customer identification
Customer due diligence must be carried out at the start of an account-based relationship. Identity may be verified through Aadhaar-based e-KYC or offline verification, officially valid documents, the Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCR), or a Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP) meeting the prescribed standards.

### 2.2 Risk categorisation
Customers must be categorised as low, medium or high risk based on identity, social and financial status, nature of business activity, and information about the customer's business and location. The risk category must be reviewed when the transaction profile is inconsistent with the customer's known profile.

### 2.3 Enhanced due diligence for PEPs
For politically exposed persons (PEPs), their family members and close associates, the entity must obtain senior management approval to establish or continue the relationship, take reasonable measures to establish the source of funds and wealth, and subject the account to enhanced ongoing monitoring.

### 2.4 Periodic updation of KYC
KYC must be periodically updated at least once every two years for high-risk customers, once every eight years for medium-risk customers and once every ten years for low-risk customers, from the date of opening the account or the last KYC updation.

## 3. Ongoing monitoring

### 3.1 Monitoring of transactions
Regulated entities must exercise ongoing due diligence commensurate with the customer's risk profile. Particular attention must be paid to complex or unusually large transactions, patterns that have no apparent economic purpose, transactions that exceed thresholds prescribed for the category of account, and high account turnover inconsistent with the size of the balance maintained or the customer's declared income.

### 3.2 Structuring and splitting of transactions
Transactions that appear to be deliberately split or structured to remain below reporting or documentation thresholds, including repeated cash deposits or wallet top-ups just below such thresholds over a short period, must be treated as a red flag and examined for possible suspicious activity.

### 3.3 Attempted transactions
Attempted transactions that are abandoned or not completed because a customer declines to furnish information must also be considered for reporting as suspicious, irrespective of the amount.

## 4. Reporting obligations to FIU-IND

### 4.1 Suspicious Transaction Report (STR)
A Suspicious Transaction Report must be furnished to FIU-IND within seven working days of arriving at a conclusion that any transaction, whether cash or non-cash, or a series of transactions integrally connected, is suspicious. The entity must not put any restriction on operations in the account merely on the basis of the STR filed.

### 4.2 Cash Transaction Report (CTR)
Cash transactions of more than Rs 10 lakh, or a series of integrally connected cash transactions that together exceed Rs 10 lakh within a month, must be reported in the Cash Transaction Report by the 15th day of the succeeding month.

### 4.3 Confidentiality and tipping-off
Directors, officers and employees are prohibited from disclosing ("tipping off") the fact that an STR or related information is being reported or provided to FIU-IND. Customers must not be informed of the filing.

### 4.4 Record keeping
Records of transactions and of identity documents obtained through customer due diligence must be maintained for at least five years after the business relationship has ended or the transaction has been completed, whichever is later, and made available to competent authorities on request.
